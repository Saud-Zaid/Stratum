Incident: adding a decision record to an architecture-decisions file silently injected the whole file header (front matter, H1, intro, a section heading) into the middle of the new entry, splitting a bullet in half.

Cause: the replacement text contained a code span that ended an anchor with a dollar sign immediately followed by a backtick. The replacer treats a dollar sign followed by a backtick as "insert everything before the match", exactly like JavaScript `String.replace`. The same family applies to a dollar sign with a single quote (text after the match), with an ampersand (the whole match) and with digits 1 to 9 (capture groups, which we use deliberately).

Signals and why they failed: the tool returned `total_replacements: 1` · `dry_run: true` reported the same single match, because the expansion happens at write time, not at match time · the follow-up `search_vault_simple` check passed, because the text it searched for was present — just in the wrong place, with 21 extra lines wedged in before it.

Detection and repair: `audit --check headings` caught it as two `R1-dup` errors (duplicated H1 and H2). `git diff HEAD` confirmed the damage was purely additive — one hunk, 18 added lines, nothing removed — so it was repaired with a second `search_and_replace` whose replacement contained no dollar sign at all, rewording the sentence instead of quoting the regex anchors.

Practical rule: when the replacement must describe a regex, describe the anchors in words, or keep them away from backticks and quotes. After any write whose replacement contains a dollar sign, verify by re-reading the section (`run_script section` or `get_vault_file_partial`), not by a substring search.
